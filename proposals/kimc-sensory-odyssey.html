Dear Ryan

Taste is where Korea begins. It need not be where it ends.

Your Culinary Odyssey opened doors that rarely open: Master Kisoondo's jars in Damyang, the monastics, the ceramicists, a table set by fifty five years of practice.

We would like to propose the next chapter. In New York, the most sought after luxury is no longer only a reservation. It is a ritual: the bathhouse, the sound session, the scent that brings you back to a place. Korea holds older and deeper versions of every one of them, and almost no one is bringing travellers to them as a single, considered journey.

The Sensory Odyssey keeps the table you have built at its heart, and wraps it in Korea's other senses: the sound of the geomungo, the scents of pine and mugwort, the weight of clay in the hand, and the stillness of a mountain temple. Your access to Korea's food masters, joined with ours to its makers of sound, scent and rest.

Doha Yoon, IEUM Concierge

I  ·  Sound

소리

Rest inside the oldest strings in Korea

The geomungo is a six string zither struck with a bamboo stick, its voice so low it is felt as much as heard. Korean court music was composed at the tempo of a resting breath, and in a darkened hall it becomes something close to meditation.

Guests lie on warm mats while master Kim Jun-young of Wacheongjae plays a few steps away. Nothing is recorded, nothing is amplified. It is a sound bath built entirely on Korean instruments, and it is already part of our journeys.

We took part in Wacheongjae's sound ritual at RAUM Social Wellness in Seoul this September. Private sessions for IEUM guests are confirmed.

II  ·  Scent

향

Bottle the air of Korea

A private workshop with an independent Seoul perfumer, composing a personal scent from notes that grow nowhere else in quite the same way: red pine after rain, wild mugwort, yuja from the southern coast, roasted green tea, Korean fir, and the hand rolled incense of the temples.

Each guest leaves with a bottle of their own, and a memory that returns every time it is opened in New York.

III  ·  Touch

촉

The shape of an empty jar

The moon jar, dalhangari, is made from two bowls joined at the waist, and is prized for the slight imperfection where they meet. Guests throw and shape their own with a ceramicist, learning a form that has held its quiet place in Korean homes for three hundred years.

Then to the onggi makers, whose breathing earthenware is the reason Korean fermentation works at all. Porous clay lets the jang breathe as it ages. It is the thread that leads straight back to your fermentation masters, and turns two workshops into one story.

IV  ·  Taste

맛

Your table, at the heart of it

This is your territory, and we would never presume to redesign it. The jang masters, royal court cuisine, sachal eumsik with the monastics, the markets at dawn: every taste chapter stays with Kim'C Market and your relationships.

What changes is everything around it. Guests arrive at the table having heard the geomungo, shaped the jar the sauce will age in, and carried the scent of the temple in with them. The meal means more because the senses around it have been woken.

V  ·  Stillness

쉼

And then, nothing at all

A night at a mountain temple: the drum before dawn, the silent monastic breakfast from four wooden bowls, tea with a monk. Tea taken on the slopes where Korean tea has grown for twelve centuries. And the hanjeungmak, Korea's pine fired stone kiln, where monks once healed the sick with heat.

The chapter that New York's bathhouses are only beginning to imitate, in the places where it began.
