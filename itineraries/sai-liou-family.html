Saturday 19 December · Day One

Namsan, Skincare, and Myeongdong

Morning

Up to Namsan Tower

While the city is still cool and the light is clearest, up to Namsan Tower for the view that makes Seoul feel like a single, sweeping thing.

Lunch

Bibimbap

At a small Michelin Guide recommended bibimbap house near Namsan.

Afternoon

Myeongdong, and Glasses for Your Daughter

Straight from lunch, out along Myeongdong's shopping streets, Korean cosmetics, fashion, and street snacks at every turn.

This is also where we sort your daughter's glasses. Korean opticians run a full eye test at no charge and have single vision lenses cut and fitted in twenty to thirty minutes, English spoken throughout, at a fraction of what the same pair costs at home.

Late Afternoon

Facial Treatment

Two options, and we'll choose closer to the trip. A facial rooted in traditional hanbang medicine, at one of Seoul's well regarded Korean herbal skincare houses. Or a non invasive treatment for hyperpigmentation at a dermatology clinic.

In Parallel

Animal Cafe, for the Family

Meanwhile, the rest of the family settles into a Myeongdong animal cafe, an open afternoon of coffee, treats, and no itinerary of their own to keep to.

Evening

Cheese Dakgalbi

Dinner together, stir fried chicken pulled through a ring of melted cheese at the table. A shared, hands on meal that tends to become everyone's favorite of the whole trip.

A note from Doha, this skincare afternoon is placed before your photography day on purpose. Skin settles beautifully in the day after a treatment, so by the time you're in hanbok at the palace, you'll look and feel your very best.

Sunday 20 December · Day Two

Hanbok, the Palace, and the Old City

Morning

Private Hanbok Fitting

Your driver brings the family to a hanbok house near the palace, where you and the children are fitted for traditional Korean dress, chosen together, colors and all.

Late Morning

Gyeongbokgung Palace

In hanbok, through Korea's grandest palace, dressed as part of the scene. You arrive for the changing of the guard at 10:00, then have the grounds at the quietest hour of a December morning.

Optional

A Private Photographer, for an Hour

A photographer who shoots inside the palace grounds regularly and knows exactly where the light falls at that hour, joining you for an hour while you are all in hanbok, with the edited images sent to you afterwards.

Lunch

Samgyetang

At a quiet, family run restaurant tucked into a hanok near the palace, known for one dish alone, whole ginseng chicken simmered until it falls off the bone.

Afternoon

Bukchon Hanok Village, and a Traditional Tea House

Narrow lanes of preserved hanok just above the palace, then a traditional tea house for sweet rice desserts and warm barley tea.

Evening

Hanwoo Korean Barbecue

Hanwoo is Korea's native breed of cattle, raised in small numbers and prized for its marbling. Dinner nearby, grilled at your own table and wrapped in lettuce leaf by leaf.

A note on why this day runs in this order. Gyeongbokgung closes at 17:00 in December, and Bukchon's residential lanes are now closed to visitors after 17:00 under the city's new rules, with a fine attached. So the palace comes before lunch and Bukchon straight after, which also places you in both for the best of the short winter light. The changing of the guard falls at 10:00, and you will be there for it.

Monday 21 December · Day Three

A Day That Belongs to the Children

10:00

Through the Gates as They Open

Lotte World opens at ten and it is fifteen minutes from your hotel, so you are inside before the queues form, which is the single thing that changes this day most. The main park sits under an enormous glass dome, warm and bright whatever December is doing outside.

11:00

Onto the Ice

There is a full ice rink three floors down, and on a December weekday it opens at eleven. Skates are hired there and the glass dome sits directly above it, so you get the daylight of an outdoor rink with none of the cold.

Lunch

Inside the Park

There is no shortage of places to eat inside the park, from snack counters to full restaurants, and my honest recommendation is to leave this one unbooked. Stop wherever suits the children at the moment they are hungry, which on a day like this will not be the hour you predicted.

Afternoon

Indoors, All of It

There is an outdoor section called Magic Island, out on the lake with the castle and the bigger rides, and I would leave it alone. Seoul in late December sits below freezing most days, and standing in a queue in that with two children ends a good day early. Everything worth doing is under the dome anyway.

If there is still time and energy afterwards, two more things sit in the same complex without going outside. The aquarium, which is entirely indoors. Or KidZania, a miniature city where children take on real jobs, a pilot, a doctor, a firefighter, and earn their own currency doing it. Its afternoon session starts at three and needs booking ahead, so tell me if you want it held.

Either

A Celebration Dinner, on the 81st Floor

You do not leave the building. Lotte World Tower rises straight out of the same complex, and on its 81st floor there are two restaurants with the whole city laid out beneath the windows. One is modern French by Yannick Alleno, with a pastry library the children are welcome to help themselves from. The other is Korean, and holds a Michelin star.

Both are formal, and worth knowing that in advance. If a proper occasion is what you want on this night, either one will give you it.

Or

Anything Else in the Tower

If a formal dinner is the last thing anyone wants after a day of rides, the floors below hold dozens of places to eat, casual and quick and easy with children. You will not be short of choice, and you still never put a coat on. Tell me if you would like me to pick a few out for you.

Tuesday 22 December · Day Four

The Museum, Then a Slow Afternoon

Morning

The National Museum of Korea, Privately Guided

An English speaking guide who works this museum often, for a couple of hours across the collections that actually reward a visit, at a pace set by the children.

Lunch

Close By

There is a food court inside the museum, which is the easy answer and perfectly good. If you would rather eat properly, tell me and I will look at what is around the museum and send you a few worth walking to.

Either

A Jjimjilbang

Korea's bathhouse tradition: hot mineral pools, kiln saunas at different heats, sleeping rooms with heated floors, and food eaten in the cotton robe they hand you at the door.

The one I would take you to is a hybrid. Baths and saunas on one side for the adults, a full indoor water park on the other for the children, so nobody spends the afternoon waiting for anyone else to finish.

One thing to know first. The bathing pools are separated by men and women, and inside them everyone is undressed. Nobody there gives it a second thought, but it is not for everyone. The water park, the robed floors, the saunas and the food are all mixed and clothed.

Or

An Afternoon Out in the City

Seongsudong is where Seoul is looking right now, old factory buildings turned into pop up stores and enormous cafes. Hongdae is the same energy, younger and louder. Both are busy, and both put you outdoors between stops, which in late December is worth weighing. The Hyundai Seoul is the warm answer, a department store built around an indoor garden, and the easiest of the three with children.

This is also the afternoon for any last skincare shopping. Korean skincare moves faster than anywhere else in the world, and what is worth carrying home changes every few months, so I will send you a current list of what is actually good before you fly.

Or

Something Slower, and Still Korean

If you would like the afternoon to stay cultural without it being another museum, I would put you in a craft workshop. Mother of pearl inlay or hanji paper making, around two hours, indoors and warm, with all four of you leaving holding something you made.

Evening

A Last Dinner, Near the Hotel

Kept close to the Park Hyatt on purpose, so the evening is short and the rest of it belongs to packing. I will find two or three good ones within a few minutes of you and book whichever you like the sound of. If there is a dish the children asked for twice this week, this is when we go back for it.
