A Sample Journey · December 2026 · Family of Four

Two Days, Imagined for Your Family

A glimpse of two days within your December stay, shaped around beauty, culture, and a pace that works for everyone.

Day One

Namsan, Skincare, and Myeongdong

A morning together, an afternoon apart, an evening together again.

Morning

Up to Namsan Tower

While the city is still cool and the light is clearest, up to Namsan Tower for the view that makes Seoul feel like a single, sweeping thing rather than a hundred separate neighborhoods.

Lunch

Bibimbap

At a small Michelin Guide recommended bibimbap house near Namsan.

Afternoon

Facial Treatment

Two directions, and we'll choose whichever suits you closer to the trip. Either a facial rooted in traditional hanbang medicine, at one of Seoul's well regarded Korean herbal skincare houses, or something more targeted, a non invasive treatment for hyperpigmentation at a dermatology clinic.

In Parallel

Animal Cafe, for the Family

Meanwhile, the rest of the family settles into a Myeongdong animal cafe, an open afternoon of coffee, treats, and no itinerary of their own to keep to.

Late Afternoon

Myeongdong, Together Again

The family reunites along Myeongdong's shopping streets, Korean cosmetics, fashion, and street snacks at every turn, easy to wander with children since there's always something new a few steps ahead.

Evening

Cheese Dakgalbi

Dinner together, stir fried chicken pulled through a ring of melted cheese at the table, the kind of hands on, slightly messy meal that tends to be everyone's favorite of the whole trip. The spice level can be adjusted to whatever your family is comfortable with, including mild enough for the children.

A note from Doha, this skincare afternoon is placed before your photography day on purpose. Skin settles beautifully in the day after a treatment, so by the time you're in hanbok at the palace, you'll look and feel your very best.

Day Two

Hanbok, the Palace, and the Old City

A day inside Korean tradition, from what you wear to where you walk.

Morning

Private Hanbok Fitting

Your driver brings the family to a hanbok house near the palace, where you and the children are fitted for traditional Korean dress, chosen together, colors and all.

Lunch

Samgyetang

At a quiet, family run restaurant tucked into a hanok near the palace, known for one dish alone, whole ginseng chicken simmered until it falls off the bone.

Afternoon

Gyeongbokgung Palace

In hanbok, through Korea's grandest palace, its courtyards and gates a backdrop few travelers ever get to walk through dressed as part of the scene rather than simply visiting it. A private photographer can join for an hour within the grounds, should you like the day to become something you carry home.

Late Afternoon

Bukchon Hanok Village, and a Traditional Tea House

Narrow lanes of preserved hanok just above the palace, then a traditional tea house for sweet rice desserts and warm barley tea, exactly the kind of pause a December afternoon in Seoul asks for.

Evening

Hanwoo Korean Barbecue

Dinner nearby, Korean beef grilled at your own table, wrapped in lettuce leaf by leaf. Interactive enough to keep the children busy and fine enough to still feel like an occasion.

And Beyond

What the Rest Could Look Like

Three more days, shaped entirely around what your family enjoys most. A few directions I would suggest.

A Theme Park Day

Largely indoors, so a cold December day never becomes the reason to cut it short.

A Museum, Privately Guided

An English speaking guide who knows how to hold a child's attention as well as yours.

A Day of Rest, at a Jjimjilbang

A full day given to Korea's traditional bathhouse culture, part hot spring, part water park for the children, and the warmest possible answer to a December in Seoul.
