Autumn Journeys

Korea in Autumn

For about six weeks a year the whole country turns, beginning in the northern ridges and finishing in the palace gardens of Seoul. Being in the right valley in the right week is the entire trip, and it is not something you can plan from a calendar.

The colour moves south, and it does not wait

Korean autumn is short and it travels. The front begins in the northern mountains around the end of September and reaches the southern temples by early November. Arriving in the right place in the wrong week is the single most common way to miss it.

Late September to mid October, the north

Seoraksan turns first. The high ridges go before the valleys, so the same mountain gives you two different weeks depending on how high you walk.

Mid to late October, the centre

Odaesan, the Gangwon valleys, and the roads between them. This is the fortnight when the country looks the way the photographs promise.

Late October to early November, the south

Naejangsan, Jirisan and the Gyeongju temples. The southern maples are the deepest red of the year, and Bulguksa under them is the image most people are carrying in their heads without knowing its name.

Early to mid November, Seoul

The city is last. The palace grounds and the ginkgo avenues hold on for a fortnight after the mountains have gone bare.

Where we go

Seoraksan

The most dramatic and the most crowded. We go early, use the less trodden approaches, and time the cable car so you are not queueing. The waterfall valleys are the reason to come, more than the summit.

Naejangsan

A wooded bowl that turns almost entirely red, reached through an avenue of maples that is itself the point. Peaks late, which makes it the safety net if the northern colour has already gone.

Gyeongju, and Bulguksa

Thousand year old temple architecture with maple against it. This is where autumn and culture stop competing for the same day.

Changdeokgung Secret Garden, Seoul

The finest autumn in the city, inside a palace, on a timed ticket with a fixed number of visitors per slot. It sells out. Securing it is a large part of what we do for this trip.

The quieter valleys

There are drives in Gangwon and North Jeolla where you will see almost nobody. They are not on any list because they have no car park and no name in English.

How this is built

Autumn is the hardest season in Korea to arrange, and the reason is simple. Everybody wants the same fortnight.

We watch the forecast and move you

The Korea Forest Service publishes a foliage front each year. We track it from September and adjust the routing, so the plan you approve in July is not the plan you travel on. This is the part that cannot be done from abroad.

Accommodation is booked far earlier than you expect

Good rooms near Seoraksan and Naejangsan in late October go many months ahead. We hold them before the front is announced and release what we do not need.

Mornings start early on purpose

Being at the trailhead an hour before the coaches is the difference between the photographs and the crowd. The driver waits, so the early start costs you nothing later.

It is a walking trip, at your pace

Nothing here requires fitness beyond a comfortable few hours. Every location has a shorter version and a longer one, and we decide on the morning.

Practical

When to book

For late October, six months ahead is comfortable and three is tight. Foliage season prices peak alongside cherry blossom.

How long

Seven to ten days lets the colour catch up with you. Under five days you are choosing one region and hoping.

What to bring

Layers. Mountain mornings are close to freezing while Seoul afternoons are mild.

With children

It works. The cable cars, the temple grounds and the shorter valley walks carry a ten year old comfortably.
