Autumn Foliage Retreat

Six weeks, moving south. The whole trip is being in the right valley in the right week.

A season to sit with

Korea's landscape is among the least known in Asia and among the most generous. For six weeks each autumn the mountains turn, valley by valley, and the best of it is found on foot in the early morning, then from a pavilion or a bench beside still water with nothing to do but look.

The days are shaped around exactly that. A long walk while the light is low, and an unhurried afternoon given over to the view, with somewhere comfortable waiting at the end of it.

Six weeks, moving south

For about six weeks a year the whole country turns, beginning on the northern ridges at the end of September and finishing in the palace gardens of Seoul in November. The colour travels south at its own pace and waits for nobody.

So this journey is built to move. We watch the front from September, and the route approved in summer is not quite the route travelled. Being in the right valley in the right week is the entire trip, and it is the part that cannot be arranged from abroad.

What the journey holds

Walking the maple valleys
Guided walks through Korea's great autumn valleys, such as Seoraksan, Naejangsan or Jirisan, each timed to the week it turns.
Temple mornings under the maples
Old mountain temples at the quietest hour, in places such as Gyeongju or the valleys of Naejangsan, with the maple against the old wood.
Palace gardens in autumn
Seoul's royal palaces at their finest, such as the Secret Garden at Changdeokgung or the grounds of Deoksugung. Some entries are timed and sell out, and holding them is part of what we do.
Forest therapy
A guided session in one of Korea's healing forests, slow walking and deep breathing under the canopy as the leaves come down.
